Lecture Note: Diagnostic Testing

Learning objective: distinguish sensitivity and specificity from post-test probabilities such as positive predictive value (PPV).

Sensitivity

The probability that the test is positive among people who truly have the disease:

P(Test + | Disease +)

Specificity

The probability that the test is negative among people who truly do not have the disease:

P(Test − | Disease −)

Positive predictive value is a different probability

PPV asks the reverse conditional question: among people who test positive, what proportion truly have the disease?

PPV = P(Disease + | Test +)

Sensitivity and PPV condition on different groups, so they are not interchangeable.

Worked example

Suppose 1,000 people are screened. Disease prevalence is 10%. The test has 90% sensitivity and 90% specificity.

Disease +Disease −
Test +90 true positives90 false positives
Test −10 false negatives810 true negatives

Among the 180 positive tests, only 90 are true positives. Therefore:

PPV = 90 / 180 = 50%

A test can have 90% sensitivity and 90% specificity while a positive result corresponds to only a 50% probability of disease in this population.
