Study Guide: Common Diagnostic Testing Mistakes

Mistake 1

“90% sensitivity means a positive result gives a 90% chance of disease.”

Incorrect. Sensitivity is P(Test + | Disease +). The probability of disease after a positive result is P(Disease + | Test +), which is PPV.

Mistake 2

“Sensitivity and specificity completely determine PPV.”

Incorrect. PPV also depends on disease prevalence (or, more generally, the pre-test probability) in the population being tested.

Mistake 3

“A positive screening test is a diagnosis.”

Not necessarily. Screening is often designed to identify people who need further evaluation or confirmatory testing.

Mistake 4

“If an AI gives the correct formula, I understand the concept.”

Not necessarily. A useful self-test is to reconstruct the 2×2 table yourself and explain why changing prevalence changes PPV.

Self-check

  1. Can you state sensitivity and PPV as conditional probabilities?
  2. Can you explain why reversing the conditional matters?
  3. Can you predict how lower prevalence affects PPV while sensitivity and specificity stay fixed?
  4. Can you build the 1,000-person table without looking at the worked example?
