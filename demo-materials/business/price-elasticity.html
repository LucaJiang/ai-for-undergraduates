Price, quantity and revenue

A shop considers changing a price. Revenue is price multiplied by quantity; it is not profit because costs are not included. Price elasticity compares proportional changes in quantity with proportional changes in price. For a finite change, the midpoint method uses the average of the two quantities and the average of the two prices as denominators.

Definition and assumptions

Midpoint elasticity = [(Q₂−Q₁)/((Q₁+Q₂)/2)] / [(P₂−P₁)/((P₁+P₂)/2)]. It is negative for these downward-sloping examples; “elastic” refers to its absolute magnitude exceeding one.

These invented before/after pairs illustrate arithmetic. They are not evidence that a price change caused the quantity change; other conditions could change too. A graph slope and elasticity are not interchangeable: elasticity also depends on the price and quantity levels.

Two checks

  1. For case A, calculate the revenue change and midpoint elasticity. What does the magnitude imply for this change?
  2. For B, does the same price reduction still raise revenue? Can either row establish what happens to profit?
