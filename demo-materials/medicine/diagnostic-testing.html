Sensitivity is not PPV

A fictional class example includes 1,000 people, of whom 100 have a disease. The test returns 180 positive results. To interpret a percentage, identify which group supplies its denominator.

Disease +Disease −
Test +9090
Test −10810

Definitions

Sensitivity is P(test positive | disease): 90 / (90 + 10) = 90%. Specificity is P(test negative | no disease): 810 / (90 + 810) = 90%. Positive predictive value (PPV) is P(disease | test positive): 90 / (90 + 90) = 50%.

Both sensitivity and PPV use the 90 true positives, but they ask different conditional questions. Equal numerators do not make the probabilities equal. At fixed sensitivity and specificity, lower prevalence increases the relative contribution of false positives among all positive tests.

Two checks

  1. Which group belongs in the denominator of sensitivity? Which group belongs in the denominator of PPV?
  2. Hold sensitivity and specificity at 90%, but change prevalence from 10% to 1%. Does PPV rise, fall or stay the same? Explain why.

This is not patient data or clinical advice. Fixed sensitivity/specificity across prevalence levels is a teaching assumption.
